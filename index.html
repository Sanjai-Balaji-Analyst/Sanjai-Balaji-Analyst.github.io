Predicting Cybersecurity Threat Severity

MSc DISSERTATION · R · CRISP-DM · HYPOTHESIS TESTING

Set out to predict cybersecurity threat severity on a 50,000-record dataset. Four algorithms, four target variables, five-fold cross-validation — sixteen configurations, all with negative R². Rather than assume a modelling mistake, I built a five-stage diagnostic protocol to test the dataset itself: distribution testing, correlation screening, an internal-consistency check (a composite score that turned out not to be a function of its own stated components), and permutation testing.

The dataset was synthetic and internally inconsistent — verifiably, not just suspiciously. The contribution is the validation protocol: runnable in under an hour, before anyone invests weeks modelling a dataset that can't support it.

Endoscopy Unit Capacity Planning

DATA DRIVEN DECISION MAKING · R (ompr / GLPK) · MILP

A hospital endoscopy unit splits 10 rooms across 26 weeks between diagnostic and therapeutic procedures, each with different setup costs, operating costs, and capacity. Formulated as a Mixed-Integer Linear Program — 1,300 variables, 780 of them binary — and solved with GLPK to minimise total cost against demand, capacity, and clinician-availability constraints.

The cost-optimal schedule leans hard on the three small rooms and leaves large rooms mostly idle — cheap, but not necessarily the utilisation pattern a hospital manager actually wants. I flagged that trade-off rather than let the optimisation output speak for itself.

HR Attrition: Tableau + KNIME

HR ANALYTICS · TABLEAU · KNIME · DECISION TREES

A Context → Tension → Resolution Tableau storyboard diagnosing attrition drivers at a pharma company — overtime, work–life balance, compensation — paired with a KNIME decision-tree classifier predicting individual attrition risk.

85% accuracy, but Cohen's Kappa of only ≈0.51 — I reported both, because attrition data is class-imbalanced and the accuracy figure alone overstates how good the model is at catching the people who actually leave.

Predictive Modelling in R: Networks & Impact

R · LASSO · GAM · LOGISTIC REGRESSION / LDA · ROC-AUC

Two linked studies testing what actually drives outcomes in collaboration and citation networks: regression, LASSO variable selection, GAMs for nonlinear effects, and classification (logistic regression vs. LDA) with full diagnostics.

Career length and network centrality consistently out-predicted the "novelty" and "elite exposure" variables the literature emphasises most — and an elite-exposure × cohesion interaction I expected to matter turned out not to be significant at all.

Airline Customer Recommendation Prediction

PYTHON · SCIKIT-LEARN · CRISP-DM · CLASSIFICATION

Predicted whether airline passengers would recommend the airline, combining structured service ratings with free-text review analysis. Compared global vs. seat-type-based imputation for missing ratings, and trained Logistic Regression and Random Forest classifiers, validated by training on some airlines and testing on entirely different ones.

Value For Money correlated with recommendation more strongly than any individual service attribute — comfort and staff service mattered less than whether customers felt the price was fair.

Insurance Data Management: Access + SQL + R

MICROSOFT ACCESS · SQL · R

Designed a relational database for an insurance company's fragmented customer/policy data, built an analytics base table in SQL using left joins (deliberately, to avoid silently dropping customers without every product), then moved into R for data-quality assessment and cleaning.

Chose to filter implausible records (negative ages, zero vehicle values) rather than impute them — imputing a fabricated value into a pricing-relevant field felt like the wrong trade-off, even at the cost of sample size.
